1 The World

The world had changed. Expanded. Stella could feel it.

There were no more rows of small rooms. There was a tower, one floor standing on another, and a sky over it that went on further than she could jump. The grey was still grey. The lights were gone, and in their place were ledges, each one lit in somebody’s colour, each one somebody’s home.

Alex was still there. Low, wide, green, quick, and still not sure about any of it.

And the small blue square from the end of the last world was there too. In the dark, before anything else had arrived, it opened its eyes.

Its name was Marcus. He was square, and he sat between them in every way: not as tall as Stella, not as wide as Alex, a jump that was neither the highest nor the lowest. He fitted places neither of them could. Alex didn’t trust him. Too perfect, he thought. Too… square.

The tower did not care what Alex thought. Its floors were built for three. Some of its ledges only Stella could climb to, some only Alex could slip onto, and some only Marcus fitted. Most of them nobody could reach alone at all.

Not every climb is made alone. Sometimes standing on someone else is the only way up.

Between floors, Stella thinks. Press fire when you have read her thoughts

2 The Cast

Stella, Alex and Marcus, drawn to scale

Stella

Tall. Red. The highest jumper in the tower and the slowest walker. A friend standing on her head reaches higher than anyone. She is the one who wonders what is past the walls, and the words between floors are hers.

Her jump lifts her a little over twice her own height. Under a low ceiling that is no help at all.

Alex

Flat. Wide. Green. The fastest of the three and the lowest jumper by a long way: his jump is less than half of Stella’s. A friend’s head turns that into something. He is the practical one, and the last to trust anybody new.

Marcus

A square. Blue. The newcomer. He walks faster than Stella and slower than Alex, and jumps higher than Alex and lower than Stella. He is shorter than Stella, so he fits under things she can’t. He is narrower than Alex, so he fits into gaps Alex can’t. Game 1 ended on him, breathing quietly under a number. This game opens on him.

3 Getting Started

Insert the cartridge with the power OFF, then switch the console ON. Use the left joystick controller, or both joysticks for two players. Press the red controller button at the title screen to begin.

Fig. 1. Your joystick controller

Joystick LEFT / RIGHTWalk
Fire buttonJump. On the title, start. On a screen of Stella’s thoughts, go on
DOWN + FireChange who you control: Stella, then Alex, then Marcus, then Stella again
UPGo through a door, or change the world (see The Tower’s Tricks)

The character you control is the one that never flickers. The other two take turns on the screen, a frame each, so they shimmer a little. That is the cartridge sharing out its sprites, and nothing is wrong.

The joystick steers in the air. A jump that is going to miss can often be pulled back.

4 How a Floor Works

Every character has a home: a ledge lit in their own colour. The floor is finished when all three are standing on their own homes at the same time. Nobody goes on ahead and nobody is left behind.

A blinking home is an empty one. The home of the character you control blinks fast. The other empty homes blink slowly. When its owner stands on it, a home holds steady and sounds that character’s note. When the third one arrives, the three notes play together and the floor is done.

Floor 1: green for Alex at the bottom, red for Stella in the middle, blue for Marcus at the top

Four things a floor will not tell you:

  • Heads are steps. Anyone can stand on anyone. Land on a friend from above; you pass through them from below or from the side. A friend does not carry you when they walk, so step off before they go.
  • The edges join. Walk off the left of the screen and you come in on the right. A ledge cut by the edge of the screen is one ledge.
  • Nothing kills you. There is nothing to fall into and nothing to die of. A bad jump costs you a walk back.
  • The sky is never solid. Everything you can stand on is lighter than the sky behind it.

5 The Tower’s Tricks

The tower teaches you three things, one act at a time, and never takes one away once you have it.

The edges (Act 1)

The screen wraps round. In the first act the way to a home is sometimes off the side of the screen and back in on the other.

Doors (Act 2)

A door is a thin column that blinks on and off. Stand in it and push UP, and you come out at the door’s other end, standing still. Only the character you control goes through. Doors work in the air as well as on the ground. A door does not always go up: some go across, and some come back down.

Two truths (Act 3)

At the very edges of the screen, from the third act on, there are two thin beams that blink: rifts. Stand in one, on the ground or on a friend’s head, and push UP, and the world changes. The tower has two truths, and a rift swaps one for the other.

You don’t move. The world does. Some ledges are true in both worlds and some in only one. When a ledge stops being true, whoever was standing on it falls. It happens to everyone at once, wherever they are, so look before you swap.

Floor 7 in the first world: dark above, light below

And in the second: the sky runs the other way

You can always tell which world you are in from the sky. In one world it is dark above and light near the ground. In the other it runs the other way.

6 The Story Game

Eleven floors in four acts. Between floors Stella thinks; press fire to go on.

It starts in the dark. Marcus is there first, alone. The sky arrives a band at a time, then he opens his eyes, and then the others come. Press fire if you want to skip it.

1. Together Again. A totem of three steps. Marcus wakes on the lowest. Alex’s home is the bottom step, drawn wide like him; Stella’s is the middle; Marcus’s is the top.

2. The Low Door. A wide block with a slab on top and a low gap between them. Stella’s home is the slab, Alex’s the block, and Marcus’s is a little ledge inside the gap.

3. The Wall. A wall from the sky to the floor down the middle of the screen, and stairs on both sides of it. Alex wakes alone on one side.

4. The Fold. A shelf across the whole screen, too high for anyone. Two doors. Every home is on the shelf or above it.

5. The High Door. One door, standing in a slot between two walls.

6. The Cross. Two doors again. Doors don’t have to go up.

7. Two Truths. The homes are in plain sight from the start, on a totem on a shelf, and nothing in this world reaches them. The rifts arrive.

8. The Handoff. Stairs climb to a perch at the edge of the screen, standing in the rift.

9. The Way Home. The same tower, but something has been exchanged.

10. The Seam. Everything happens at the edge of the screen, where the two sides meet. The doors stand in only one world.

11. Where They Meet. The first floor’s totem again, upside down, true in only one world. The hardest floor in the game.

When the last floor is done there is a number. It is your time, in seconds, for the whole climb. Lower is better. The clock only runs while a floor is on the screen; reading Stella’s thoughts and watching the opening are free.

7 The Sky

The tower has a sky, and it is never quite the same twice.

When the three of them set out, it is nearly all dark: black overhead, a little grey at the edge of the world. Stella took it for the end of a day. She had only ever known the end of things.

It was not dusk. Every time they climb higher together, the grey climbs too. By the top of the tower the light has nearly filled the sky, and the dark is a thin strip overhead. It had been the hour before dawn all along.

8 The Music

In the last world Stella had one tune and one voice to play it. It is still here. Underneath it now there is a second voice, holding up each phrase.

Each of them has a note. Marcus’s is the lowest, then Stella’s, then Alex’s. Get somebody home and you hear theirs. Get all three home and you hear them together.

You can hear who jumped, too.

9 The Endless Game

At the title screen, press SELECT until the sky glows ember. Then press fire.

Floors arrive at random from all eleven, never the same one twice running, with no thoughts in between. The first has 45 seconds. Every floor you finish takes two seconds off the next, and the clock never drops below twelve. When the clock wins, the screen shows how many floors you finished and how long you lasted.

10 The Clock

The story game has no clock. Switch the left difficulty to A and every floor gets 45 seconds, starting from the next floor. Nothing on the screen counts down. In the last sixteen seconds the music plays twice as fast and the sky goes dark, a step at a time. At zero the floor starts again from the beginning, and the time of your climb keeps counting.

A few seconds left. The sky goes dark, not red: in this tower, colour belongs to the three of them

The ember sky: the endless game

With the switch at B there is no limit.

11 Two Players

Press SELECT at the title once and Alex appears beside Stella: that is the two-player game. The second player uses the right joystick. Player one starts each floor as Stella and player two as Alex. DOWN + Fire takes Marcus, or whoever nobody is holding. Both players’ homes blink fast.

Two players can do something one cannot: two people can move at once. Nothing in the tower needs it. All of it can be done alone, one character at a time.

13 Tips from the Programmer

  • Stuck? The fast-blinking home is the one belonging to whoever you are controlling. Every answer is at the back, behind a warning.
  • A home above its owner’s jump wants a friend underneath. Ask who has to stand still, and for how long.
  • Steer. A jump can be pulled in the air, and a long jump off a friend’s head goes further than it looks.
  • Before you change worlds, look at where everybody is standing. A swap takes everyone with it.
  • The edge of the screen is not a wall. On the last two floors it is the middle of everything.

14 About This Cartridge

Made the 8K way

Game 1 kept to the rules of 1977: 4,096 bytes and nothing else. This game is the next step the real 2600 took. In 1981 Atari could not fit its home version of Asteroids into 4K, so the cartridge carried 8K in two halves and switched between them. That trick is called bank switching, and this cartridge uses the same scheme, F8.

So there is twice the room. It went on the things a game of 1981 would spend it on: a title screen, a story told between floors, a second player, a second voice in the music, and a world that joins at the edges and has two truths. The console is the same one. It still has 128 bytes of memory, and the options are still the console’s own switches, as they were.

One half of the cartridge plays the game. The other holds the words, the title, the music and the sky. The game calls on it every frame for the music, and whenever the sky needs painting.

From the first game

The blue square under the number at the end of Stella Was Alone is Marcus. This game opens on the same square, in the dark, and wakes it.

Stella and Alex are the same size and the same colours as before, down to the byte. Standing on a friend’s head works as it did. The music is the first game’s tune, note for note, with a second voice under it now. The endless game keeps the first game’s ember title and its clock, 45 seconds falling to twelve.

What changed: a lit pad became a home you stand on, the rooms became a tower, the edges join, there are doors and two worlds, and the world got a sky that changes as you climb.

How this game was made

The story, the characters and the series are Michael Borck’s. The code, the floor designs, the test tools and much of the writing in this booklet were developed with the help of generative AI models, working under his direction. He reviewed, edited and approved all of it, and decided what went into the game.

Programs written for this project check the game on every build. One replays the game’s own physics to prove that every floor can be finished, and that each floor’s trick can’t be skipped. One runs the cartridge in a software model of the console: it plays the story through, plays the hardest floors move by move, listens to the music, and checks the timing of every line the television draws. One draws this booklet’s pictures from the cartridge’s own frames. None of them is inside the cartridge.

This page is here because a player should be able to know how a thing they enjoy was made. The tools did a great deal of the work. The choices, and the responsibility for them, are his.

The source

The whole game is open: the source, the tools, the design documents, and a record of every design decision and why it was made, at github.com/retroverse-studios/stellas-evolution.

15 Behind the Design

Colour means somebody

The tower is grey. The sky is grey, the ledges are grey, the doors and rifts are grey. The only colours on the screen are the three characters and their homes. A home is in colour because a home is what someone wants. When the series’ third game needs something to be wrong, it will break this rule.

The order of brightness never changes either: Alex brightest, Stella in the middle, Marcus darkest, whoever you are controlling. On a black-and-white set, or to a player who can’t tell red from green, the three are still three.

The hour before dawn

Each act has its own sky, a little lighter than the last. The black at the top never quite goes. In the other world the sky runs backwards, and the black that was overhead ends up at your feet; if it were any lighter there, Marcus would vanish against it.

Every floor is proved before it ships

A program replays the game’s physics, steering in the air as you can, and must find a way home for every character on every floor or the build fails. It also proves the negatives: that floor 3 can’t be done without the edges, floor 4 without the doors, floor 7 without the second world, and so on. A floor’s trick is never optional.

That program can be too generous. It imagines a friend standing wherever that friend could ever get to, all at once. For floors 10 and 11 that isn’t good enough, so a second program plays them in the cartridge itself, a move at a time, and checks that the order in the walkthrough is real. It also checks that the shortcuts don’t work.

Floor 11 is in order

The world swap is for everyone. On the last floor the totem is true only in the first world, so once anybody is home another swap would drop them off it. All the swapping comes first, and after the last swap the three of them are standing on one small stone. From there Stella can only get up from Alex’s head, so she goes while he is still on the stone. Alex can make his leap alone. Marcus can only get in from Alex’s head once Alex is home. Stella, Alex, Marcus: one order.

Floor 11 in the first world: the totem and the stone

And in the second: the walkway, and no totem

The edges

The screen has always wrapped round in this game. Act 1 makes you use it. By Act 4 a ledge, a rift and a door all stand on the seam at once, and the seam is where the floor is.

Two halves

The cartridge is two 4K banks. Play runs in one. The other holds the title, every word Stella thinks, the music, the sky painter and most of the opening. During play the game switches over to it for the music once a frame, and for the sky whenever it changes, and straight back.

Eight kilobytes

All of it fits in 8,192 bytes. There are 71 bytes to spare in the half that plays the game, and 805 in the other.

16 Walkthrough

Printed upside down, so you only read it on purpose.

Heights are the tower’s: up is up. “The rift” means the thin beam at either edge of the screen.

1. Together Again. Stella stands under the totem and jumps onto the middle step. Marcus hops up the steps to the top. Alex can’t reach the bottom step alone: he jumps onto a friend’s head on the floor and from there onto the step.

2. The Low Door. Stella jumps onto the block, then onto the slab. Alex jumps onto the block from a friend’s head. Marcus climbs onto the block, walks into the gap, and hops up onto the little ledge inside. Stella is too tall for the gap.

3. The Wall. Alex can’t reach his stair alone, and his friends are on the other side of the wall. Walk one of them off the edge of the screen; they come in on Alex’s side. They stand on the bottom stair and Alex climbs them to his. Stella climbs her stairs one at a time, and Marcus hops onto the bottom one.

4. The Fold. Everyone stands in the left door on the floor and pushes UP. It lets out on the shelf, which is Alex’s home. Marcus jumps onto the short ledge nearest the door. Stella jumps up to the high ledge in the middle. The right door comes back down to the floor.

5. The High Door. Alex’s home is the top of a wall beside the door; he gets there from a friend’s head and never uses the door. Stella and Marcus jump onto a wall, drop into the slot, and push UP. The door lets out on the shelf, which is Marcus’s home. Stella jumps from the shelf to her ledge.

6. The Cross. The doors cross over. Alex takes the left door, which puts him straight onto his ledge. Marcus takes the right door onto the shelf, his home. Stella takes the right door too, and jumps from the shelf up to her perch.

7. Two Truths. Walk into the rift on the floor and push UP. In the other world there are two rungs on the left. Climb them to the shelf, and from the shelf the totem: Marcus to the middle step, Stella to the top. Alex’s home is the shelf, but every rung is too high for him: he climbs a friend at each one.

8. The Handoff. Everyone climbs the stairs to the perch at the edge. When all three are up, one of them stands in the rift and pushes UP. The stairs are gone in the other world, which is why nobody can be left on them. The perch is Alex’s home. Marcus and Stella climb the new ledges above it.

9. The Way Home. Push UP in the rift on the floor. The stairs are in the other world this time, and so is the perch, but the homes are not: when you get there they vanish. Climb to the perch anyway, all three, and swap back from it. The homes come back around you. Alex is home; Marcus and Stella climb to theirs.

10. The Seam. All three homes are in the second world. On the floor, walk into the rift and push UP. In the second world the doors are there: stand in one and push UP, for everyone. You come out on the seam ledge, the ledge cut in half by the edge of the screen. Swap back from the seam ledge: stand at its far right end, in the rift but past the door, or UP takes the door instead. In the first world there is a high ledge above the seam ledge. Alex climbs onto Stella’s head and from there onto it, and Stella jumps up after him. Marcus waits on the seam ledge, at its right-hand end. Stella walks to the right along the high ledge, nearly to its end. Alex climbs onto her head so that he sticks out past her into the rift, and pushes UP. Stella drops home onto the seam ledge, and Alex lands on his. Stella stays at the left end of her home. Marcus climbs onto her head, then takes a long jump left, steering all the way, onto the ledge in the middle. From the bare seam ledge it can’t be done.

11. Where They Meet. The totem is true only in the first world. Walk into the rift on the floor and push UP. In the second world, each of them takes the left door. It lets out on a walkway high on the right. All three walk right to the very end, into the rift. Push UP. The walkway vanishes and everyone lands together on the small stone at the edge. Now the order matters. Alex stands at the inner end of the stone. Stella climbs onto his head and leaps left onto the top of the totem. Alex leaps left onto the bottom step, his home, on his own. Alex walks to the right end of his step. Marcus leaps from the stone onto Alex’s head, then hops up to the middle step. Stella’s ledge is in the way from anywhere else.

Every floor in this cartridge is checked by a program before the cartridge is built. If you are stuck, the floor is not the problem.

The pictures in this booklet are not photographs of a television. They are frames of the game, drawn from what the cartridge itself sends to the screen.
